\title{Exact Soundness of the Multipartite Product Test}
\author{}
\date{July 13, 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The product test is the optimal two-copy test with perfect completeness for
multipartite pure product states.  Let $\PT_n(\omega)$ be its largest
acceptance probability over $n$-partite pure states whose squared overlap
with the closest product state is $\omega$, with finite local dimensions
allowed to vary.  Soleimanifar and Wright determined $\PT_n(\omega)$ for
$\omega\geq 1/2$, while Lovitz and Lowe recently determined the bipartite
curve and asked whether it remains extremal in the multipartite low-overlap
regime.  We prove that the answer is yes.  If
$k=\lfloor 1/\omega\rfloor$, then for every $n\geq2$ and
$0<\omega\leq1$,
\[
  \PT_n(\omega)
  =\frac12\left(1+k\omega^2+(1-k\omega)^2\right).
\]
Consequently, the soundness tends to $1/2$ as $\omega$ tends to zero, and
an extremal state can always be chosen as a bipartite Schmidt state tensored
with product factors.  Thus the formula also gives the exact soundness curve
of the best two-copy, perfect-completeness test.  The proof is a one-party
Schmidt-decomposition induction, inspired by the SVD analysis of the swap
norm by Bhangale, Khot, Liu, and Minzer.  A sharp bound on off-diagonal
Schmidt terms reduces the induction to an elementary capped-purity
optimization, whose branches combine by concatenating probability vectors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let
\[
  \ket{\psi}\in \mathcal H_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal H_n
\]
be a multipartite pure state.  The product test takes two copies of
$\ket\psi$, performs a swap test between the two copies of each local
system, and accepts if every local test accepts.  If $F_i$ swaps the two
copies of $\mathcal H_i$, its acceptance projector is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-projector-intro}
  Q_n=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n \frac{\id+F_i}{2}.
\end{equation}
Every product state is accepted with probability one.  The parameter that
governs soundness is the squared closest-product overlap
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega-intro}
  \omega(\psi)
  =\max_{\substack{\ket{\phi_i}\in\mathcal H_i\\
                      \|\phi_i\|=1}}
    \left|\braket{\phi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\phi_n}{\psi}\right|^2.
\end{equation}
Its negative logarithm is one standard convention for the geometric
measure of entanglement~\cite{WeiGoldbart03}.

The two-register swap test already appears in quantum
fingerprinting~\cite{BuhrmanEtAl01}, and closely related products of local
symmetrizers occur in observable constructions for multipartite
concurrence~\cite{MintertKusBuchleitner05}.  Harrow and
Montanaro~\cite{HarrowMontanaro13} established
dimension- and party-independent soundness bounds for the product test and
proved a pointwise optimality statement: among all tests on two copies that
accept every pure product state with certainty, the product test rejects
every input as often as possible.  Their estimates show that the acceptance
probability is $1-\Theta(1-\omega)$, but the constants in the regime of
small $\omega$ were left open.  Soleimanifar and
Wright~\cite{SoleimanifarWright22} formalized the acceptance probability as
an extremal function of the overlap.  We make the dimension convention in
their dimension-suppressed notation explicit by defining
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PT-definition-intro}
  \PT_n(\omega)
  =\sup_{\substack{d_1,\ldots,d_n\in\mathbb N,\\
                    \ket\psi\in\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\C^{d_i},\\
                    \|\psi\|=1,\ \omega(\psi)=\omega}}
       \bra{\psi}^{\otimes2}Q_n\ket{\psi}^{\otimes2},
\end{equation}
where the supremum ranges over all finite local dimensions.  They proved
\[
  \PT_n(\omega)=1-\omega+\omega^2
  \qquad (n\geq2,\ \omega\geq1/2),
\]
using a recursive Schmidt-decomposition analysis.  For $\omega<1/2$ they
obtained the upper bound $2/3+\omega^2/3$, left the exact value open, and
asked in particular whether $\PT_n(\omega)$ tends to $1/2$ as
$\omega\to0$.

Lovitz and Lowe~\cite[Corollary~6.3]{LovitzLowe26} subsequently computed
the exact bipartite curve.  In the notation below, their value is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LL-candidate}
  \frac12\left(1+k\omega^2+(1-k\omega)^2\right),
  \qquad k=\left\lfloor\frac1\omega\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Padding their bipartite optimizer with product factors gives the same
expression as a lower bound for every $n\geq2$.  They asked explicitly
whether this bound is tight for $n>2$ and $\omega<1/2$
\cite[Remark~6.4]{LovitzLowe26}.  Our main result proves the matching
multipartite upper bound.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $n\geq2$ and $0<\omega\leq1$, let
\[
  k=\left\lfloor\frac1\omega\right\rfloor,
  \qquad r=1-k\omega.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-curve}
  \PT_n(\omega)=\frac12\left(1+k\omega^2+r^2\right).
\end{equation}
More strongly, for every fixed finite choice of local Hilbert spaces and
every state $\ket\psi$ on them,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-dimensional-upper}
  \bra{\psi}^{\otimes2}Q_n\ket{\psi}^{\otimes2}
  \leq
  \frac12\left(1+k\omega(\psi)^2+
                   (1-k\omega(\psi))^2\right),
  \qquad k=\left\lfloor\frac1{\omega(\psi)}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
If two local dimensions are at least $\lceil1/\omega\rceil$, equality is
attained by a state entangled only across those two systems.
\end{theorem}

The proof sharpens the existing Schmidt recursion in two ways.  Decompose
$\ket\psi$ across one party versus the rest, and retain every Schmidt
branch.  The product-test acceptance has an exact recurrence in which an
off-diagonal pair of orthogonal Schmidt vectors contributes at most $1/2$.
The diagonal branches are controlled inductively.  Their product overlaps
need not be bounded individually by $\omega$; instead, if $\lambda_a$ is a
Schmidt weight and $\omega_a$ is the product overlap inside its branch,
then $\lambda_a\omega_a\leq\omega$.  Each branch produces a probability
vector capped by $\omega_a$.  Scaling it by $\lambda_a$ and concatenating
all the vectors gives a single probability distribution capped by
$\omega$.  Maximizing the purity of such a distribution yields exactly the
piecewise-quadratic expression in~\eqref{eq:main-curve}.

The organization of this proof was suggested by the swap-norm viewpoint of
Bhangale, Khot, Liu, and Minzer~\cite{BKLM24}.  Their coordinatewise swap
form is the same quartic expression as the product-test acceptance after a
function is normalized as a quantum amplitude tensor, and their
$J$-SVD is the corresponding Schmidt decomposition.  Their high-swap
argument peels off one SVD coordinate at a time, while their general inverse
theorem uses random restrictions and common refinements of several SVDs.
We use none of that deeper machinery: the positive projector in the quantum
test gives the sharp off-diagonal constant $1/2$, and capped-purity
concatenation closes the recursion directly.  Recent work has also related
multi-copy product tests to convergent hierarchies for the geometric measure
of entanglement~\cite{WeinbrennerEtAl26}; the present result concerns the
exact extremal behavior of the original two-copy test.

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} records the swap-form identity and the
scalar capped-purity lemma.  Section~\ref{sec:soundness} proves the exact
upper and lower bounds.  Section~\ref{sec:consequences} gives the limiting,
structural, and testing consequences.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{The product test and the dimension convention}

All Hilbert spaces in this paper are finite-dimensional.  On two copies of
$\mathcal H_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal H_n$, let $F_i$ swap the two
copies of the $i$th local system.  The operators $F_i$ commute, so
$Q_n$ in~\eqref{eq:product-projector-intro} is an orthogonal projector.
We write
\[
  p_n(\psi)=\bra{\psi}^{\otimes2}Q_n\ket{\psi}^{\otimes2}.
\]
The maximum in~\eqref{eq:omega-intro} is attained by compactness.  A
nonzero finite-dimensional pure state always has positive product overlap:
some nonzero coefficient in any product basis supplies a product vector
with nonzero overlap.  Thus exact statements use $\omega>0$; the value at
$\omega=0$ is only a limit.

We follow the convention of Soleimanifar and Wright in
\eqref{eq:PT-definition-intro}: local dimensions are not parameters of
$\PT_n$.  The upper bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is statewise and hence
holds for fixed dimensions.  The matching construction may require larger
spaces, specifically two local dimensions at least
$\lceil1/\omega\rceil$.

\subsection{The classical swap form}

We spell out the normalization behind the connection to~\cite{BKLM24}.
Let $\Sigma$ be a finite set, let $f:\Sigma^n\to\C$ be nonzero, and use
the uniform norm
\[
  \|f\|_{2,\mathrm{unif}}^2
  =\E_{x\in\Sigma^n}|f(x)|^2.
\]
Associate to $f$ the normalized amplitude state
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amplitude-state}
  \ket{\psi_f}
  =\frac{1}{|\Sigma|^{n/2}\|f\|_{2,\mathrm{unif}}}
    \sum_{x\in\Sigma^n} f(x)\ket{x_1}\cdots\ket{x_n}.
\end{equation}
For independent uniform $x,y\in\Sigma^n$, independently preserve or swap
the pair $(x_i,y_i)$ in each coordinate, and denote the resulting pair by
$(x',y')$.  The BKLM swap form is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:swap-form}
  \swap(f)
  =\E_{x,y,(x',y')}
     \left[f(x)f(y)\overline{f(x')f(y')}\right].
\end{equation}
Expanding $Q_n=2^{-n}\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}F_S$, where $F_S$ swaps exactly
the coordinates in $S$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:swap-quantum-identity}
  p_n(\psi_f)
  =\frac{\swap(f)}{\|f\|_{2,\mathrm{unif}}^4}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the summand for $S$ is the normalized average of
$\overline{f(x)f(y)}f(x')f(y')$; the coordinate-swap involution exchanges
this expression with the one in~\eqref{eq:swap-form}.  This also makes clear
that complex conjugations and the uniform normalization are essential.

Under~\eqref{eq:amplitude-state}, a product function becomes a product-state
amplitude tensor after its factors are normalized.  Similarly, a matrix SVD
of $f$ across coordinates $J$ and $[n]\setminus J$ is, up to the harmless
uniform-versus-counting normalization and the usual conjugation of the
right singular vector, a Schmidt decomposition across the same
bipartition.  Identity~\eqref{eq:swap-quantum-identity} is motivational; the
proof below is stated entirely in quantum language.

\subsection{Capped purity}

The scalar function controlling the soundness curve is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h-def}
  h(t)=\max\left\{
       \sum_j p_j^2:
       p_j\geq0,\ \sum_jp_j=1,\ \max_jp_j\leq t
     \right\},
  \qquad 0<t\leq1,
\end{equation}
where arbitrary finite probability vectors and trailing zeroes are allowed.

\begin{lemma}[Capped purity]\label{lem:capped-purity}
If $k=\lfloor1/t\rfloor$ and $s=1-kt$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h-formula}
  h(t)=kt^2+s^2.
\end{equation}
An optimizer has $k$ entries equal to $t$, one entry equal to $s$ when
$s>0$, and all remaining entries zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose two positive coordinates $p_i\geq p_j$ are both strictly below
$t$.  Put $\delta=\min\{t-p_i,p_j\}$ and replace them by
$p_i+\delta$ and $p_j-\delta$.  The cap and total mass are preserved, while
the change in purity is
\[
  (p_i+\delta)^2+(p_j-\delta)^2-p_i^2-p_j^2
  =2\delta(p_i-p_j)+2\delta^2>0.
\]
Repeating this transfer leaves at most one positive coordinate below the
cap.  The unit total mass then forces exactly $k$ capped entries and the
residual $s=1-kt$.  If $t=1/m$, then $k=m$ and $s=0$; this is the
reciprocal-integer boundary.  The resulting vector is feasible and has the
purity in~\eqref{eq:h-formula}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact soundness}\label{sec:soundness}

We first isolate the exact recurrence and the off-diagonal estimate.

\begin{lemma}[One-party Schmidt recurrence]\label{lem:recurrence}
Let $n\geq2$ and Schmidt-decompose the first party from the rest as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schmidt}
  \ket\psi=\sum_a\sqrt{\lambda_a}\,\ket a\ket{v_a},
\end{equation}
where the positive $\lambda_a$ sum to one and the $\ket{v_a}$ are
orthonormal.  Define
\[
  Q_R=\bigotimes_{i=2}^n\frac{\id+F_i}{2},
  \qquad
  q_{ab}=\bra{v_a}\bra{v_b}Q_R\ket{v_a}\ket{v_b}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
  p_n(\psi)=\sum_{a,b}\lambda_a\lambda_b q_{ab}.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-bounds}
  q_{aa}=p_{n-1}(v_a),
  \qquad
  q_{ab}\leq\frac12\quad(a\neq b).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $F_R=\prod_{i=2}^nF_i$ be the global swap of the two copies of all
remaining parties.  Since $Q_R$ projects onto the simultaneous $+1$
eigenspaces of the local swaps,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FR-identities}
  F_RQ_R=Q_R,
  \qquad
  Q_R\preceq\frac{\id+F_R}{2}.
\end{equation}
The second relation is the corresponding containment of projector ranges.

Expanding the first local symmetric projector in
$Q_n=((\id+F_1)/2)Q_R$, the identity term contributes
\[
  \frac12\sum_{a,b}\lambda_a\lambda_b q_{ab}.
\]
The first-party swap term contributes
\[
  \frac12\sum_{a,b}\lambda_a\lambda_b
  \bra{v_b}\bra{v_a}Q_R\ket{v_a}\ket{v_b}.
\]
But $\bra{v_b}\bra{v_a}=\bra{v_a}\bra{v_b}F_R$, so the first relation
in~\eqref{eq:FR-identities} makes this cross matrix element equal to
$q_{ab}$.  This proves~\eqref{eq:recurrence}.

The diagonal identity in~\eqref{eq:q-bounds} is the definition of the
product test on the remaining parties.  For $a\neq b$, the second relation
in~\eqref{eq:FR-identities} and orthogonality give
\begin{align*}
  q_{ab}
  &\leq
  \bra{v_a}\bra{v_b}\frac{\id+F_R}{2}
      \ket{v_a}\ket{v_b} \\
  &=\frac12\left(1+|\braket{v_a}{v_b}|^2\right)
   =\frac12.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Statewise upper bound]\label{prop:upper}
For every $n\geq1$ and every $n$-partite pure state $\ket\psi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:statewise-h}
  p_n(\psi)\leq\frac{1+h(\omega(\psi))}{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We use induction on $n$.  For $n=1$, every pure state is a product state,
so $p_1(\psi)=\omega(\psi)=h(1)=1$.

Let $n\geq2$, use~\eqref{eq:schmidt}, and for each branch write
\[
  \omega_a
  =\max_{\ket\pi\text{ product on parties }2,\ldots,n}
       |\braket{\pi}{v_a}|^2.
\]
Choosing $\ket a$ on the first party and a closest product vector to
$\ket{v_a}$ on the other parties shows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:branch-cap}
  \lambda_a\omega_a\leq\omega(\psi)=:\omega
  \qquad\text{for every }a.
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:recurrence}, its off-diagonal bound, and the inductive
hypothesis,
\begin{align}
  p_n(\psi)
  &\leq
    \sum_a\lambda_a^2\frac{1+h(\omega_a)}{2}
    +\frac12\sum_{a\neq b}\lambda_a\lambda_b \notag\\
  &=\frac12+\frac12\sum_a\lambda_a^2h(\omega_a).
  \label{eq:induction-before-concat}
\end{align}

It remains to compare the last sum with $h(\omega)$.  For every $a$, let
$q^{(a)}$ be a finite probability vector attaining $h(\omega_a)$ in
Lemma~\ref{lem:capped-purity}, and concatenate the scaled vectors by setting
\begin{equation}\label{eq:concatenation}
  p_{a,j}=\lambda_a q_j^{(a)}.
\end{equation}
The $p_{a,j}$ form a probability distribution.  By
\eqref{eq:branch-cap}, every atom satisfies
\[
  p_{a,j}\leq\lambda_a\omega_a\leq\omega.
\]
Its purity is exactly
\[
  \sum_{a,j}p_{a,j}^2
  =\sum_a\lambda_a^2\sum_j(q_j^{(a)})^2
  =\sum_a\lambda_a^2h(\omega_a).
\]
The definition of $h(\omega)$ therefore bounds this quantity by
$h(\omega)$.  Substitution in~\eqref{eq:induction-before-concat} proves
\eqref{eq:statewise-h}.
\end{proof}

The concatenation in~\eqref{eq:concatenation} is the closure step that is
lost if one keeps only the leading Schmidt branch.  It uses the full family
of constraints $\lambda_a\omega_a\leq\omega$ without requiring either
$\lambda_a$ or $\omega_a$ separately to be at most $\omega$.

\begin{proposition}[Bipartite extremizer]\label{prop:lower}
Let $n\geq2$ and $0<\omega\leq1$.  Put
$k=\lfloor1/\omega\rfloor$ and $r=1-k\omega$.  If two local dimensions are
at least $\lceil1/\omega\rceil$, there is a state $\ket{\Psi_\omega}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-properties}
  \omega(\Psi_\omega)=\omega,
  \qquad
  p_n(\Psi_\omega)=\frac12(1+k\omega^2+r^2).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take Schmidt weights
\[
  p_1=\cdots=p_k=\omega,
  \qquad p_{k+1}=r\quad\text{if }r>0,
\]
and on two parties define
\[
  \ket{\Psi_\omega^{(2)}}=\sum_j\sqrt{p_j}\,\ket j\ket j.
\]
The required Schmidt rank is $k$ when $r=0$ and $k+1$ otherwise, namely
$\lceil1/\omega\rceil$.  The closest-product overlap of a bipartite pure
state is its largest Schmidt weight, so it is exactly $\omega$.

For completeness, if $\rho_A$ and $\rho_B$ are the two marginals, expansion
of the four terms in
$Q_2=((\id+F_A)/2)((\id+F_B)/2)$ gives
\[
  p_2(\Psi_\omega^{(2)})
  =\frac14\left(1+\Tr\rho_A^2+\Tr\rho_B^2+1\right)
  =\frac12\left(1+\sum_jp_j^2\right).
\]
The final sum is $k\omega^2+r^2$.  Tensoring arbitrary pure product states
on the other $n-2$ parties preserves both the closest-product overlap and
the acceptance probability.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}, Lemma~\ref{lem:capped-purity}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:lower} prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Consequences and discussion}\label{sec:consequences}

\paragraph{The high-overlap regime and reciprocal breakpoints.}
For $\omega>1/2$, one has $k=1$, and~\eqref{eq:main-curve} becomes
\[
  \PT_n(\omega)=1-\omega+\omega^2.
\]
At $\omega=1/2$, the floor convention gives $k=2$, but the value is still
$3/4$, so the same formula holds on the closed interval
$[1/2,1]$.  More generally, the curve is continuous and quadratic between
the breakpoints $\omega=1/m$.  At such a breakpoint, the residual vanishes;
equivalently, it may be viewed as one additional capped atom, so the two
neighboring quadratic descriptions agree.

\paragraph{The low-overlap limit.}
Since $k\omega^2\leq\omega$ and $0\leq r<\omega$, Lemma
\ref{lem:capped-purity} gives
\[
  0\leq h(\omega)\leq\omega+\omega^2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
  \lim_{\omega\to0}\PT_n(\omega)=\frac12,
\]
answering the question of Soleimanifar--Wright and the multipartite
tightness question of Lovitz--Lowe.  The formula is independent of the
number of parties.

\paragraph{Bipartite extremality.}
For every prescribed $\omega$, an optimizer in the dimension-free problem
can be chosen to have entanglement on only two parties.  Thus genuinely
multipartite entanglement provides no strict advantage in maximizing false
acceptance at fixed closest-product overlap.  This is an existence
statement about an optimizer; the proof does not classify every equality
case.  A natural next question is a stability version: whether every
near-extremizer must be close, in an appropriate sense, to a state with a
similarly capped Schmidt spectrum.

\paragraph{Optimality among two-copy tests.}
Let $M$ be the accepting POVM element of any two-copy test having perfect
completeness for all pure product states.  The vectors
\[
  \left\{
    (\ket{\phi_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\ket{\phi_n})^{\otimes2}
  \right\}
\]
span $\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathcal H_i)$, whose projector is
exactly $Q_n$.  Perfect completeness and $0\preceq M\preceq\id$ imply
$M\succeq Q_n$; this is the argument of
Harrow--Montanaro~\cite[Section~5]{HarrowMontanaro13}.  Hence every such
test accepts every input at least as often as the product test.  Theorem
\ref{thm:main} therefore gives the exact best possible two-copy,
perfect-completeness soundness curve, not only the performance of one
particular measurement.

Higher-copy perfect-completeness tests project each local collection of
copies onto its symmetric subspace~\cite{HarrowMontanaro13}, and recent
hierarchies study their relation to closest-product overlap
\cite{WeinbrennerEtAl26}.  Determining comparably sharp finite-copy curves
beyond two copies remains an interesting direction.
